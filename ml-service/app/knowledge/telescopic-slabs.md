# How telescopic slab tariffs work

Domestic electricity in India is usually billed with telescopic (progressive) slabs. Each slab has its own rate, and
only the units that fall inside a slab are charged at that slab's rate. Crossing into a higher slab does not
re-price the earlier units. For example, with slabs of 0-100, 101-300, 301-500 and above 500 kWh, a household that
uses 320 kWh pays the first-slab rate for 100 units, the second-slab rate for 200 units and the third-slab rate for
the last 20 units.

Bills rise faster than consumption when usage moves into a higher slab, because every extra unit costs more. Summer
air conditioning and new appliances are the most common reasons for crossing a slab boundary.
